A task that follows the work

Today a task shows a state and, at the end, an outcome. It does not show the work in between. This page measures the gap, shows what a live task could look like, and compares six ways to build it.

Short answer

Add a Live block, fed by three writers with three levels of trust.

  • The page is static because it has two grains only: a one-line state and a final outcome. The steps in between have no place.
  • The harness already records every step. Almost none of it reaches the task.
  • Build one Live block on a working task: what is happening now, how far along the plan is, and what happened so far.
  • Fill it first with facts the harness records, for no tokens. Then with a short plan from the worker. Then with news lines from a small model.
  • Keep the three kinds apart on the page: recorded facts, the agent's own words, and automatic summaries.
  • A small model that reads all the work of this project would cost about 5 cents a day. Keeping reports current costs the big models several dollars a day now.

Why the page feels static

  • Two grains, nothing between. Now is one line that replaces itself. Results describe the end. Steps have no place.
  • Now is thin. A phase has three words at the median and stays 6.6 minutes. Old phases leave no trail, by design.
  • Workers write when they finish a chunk. They forget exactly when they are deepest in the work.
  • The coordinator's view goes to the chat. 284 check-ins led to 247 chat replies and 5 report writes.
  • The page hides what moves. Activity is folded and lists only starts and reports. Nothing marks what is new since the last look.
  • No sense of distance. Nothing says how far along the work is.

What exists today

  • Automatic Now. A reducer picks one line from durable facts. It costs nothing and is always true. It carries a state, not a story.
  • Stale marks and nudges. When facts pass a report by, the coordinator is asked to rewrite it. 312 such turns cost about 41 dollars, 16% of coordinator spend.
  • The small-model check. It judges whether a report is out of date. It never writes. 76 calls in eight days cost just over one cent.
  • Worker progress calls. 419 of 443 stood alone in their request, at about 100,000 tokens of context: roughly 2 cents each.
  • Check-ins. Every ten minutes a coordinator reads a digest of its workers. That digest is the raw material a watcher needs.

Who writes, and how far to trust it

Three writers

  • The harness, from events. The pulse, the counters, and facts: a commit, a recorded check, saved files, work sent back, a failure. No tokens. It cannot say why.
  • The worker, in passing. A plan of three to seven steps, ticked as it goes, and a few words of intent on a command. No extra request when it rides on a call it makes anyway.
  • A small model, watching. One news sentence for every 8 to 30 steps. It can tick the plan from evidence and flag a stuck worker.
  • The coordinator keeps the outcome sections. What it decides about the work (checked, sent back) shows as facts.

  • Auto: a small model read the work log. Tap it to see the steps it was made from.
  • Auto text never enters What you get, Not done or Next. It never puts a task under Needs you.
  • "Passed", "committed" and "saved" come from recorded facts only.

Six approaches

A to D stack: each adds a layer to the same Live block. E and F build on D. Effort is for normal workers, web and Android included where the page changes.

A · Show what we have

Keep this run's phases and result lines as a trail. Show them in a Live block with a "new since you looked" mark.

Effort: 3 to 4 days. Tokens: none. Risk: the quiet stretches stay, because workers still write four times a run.

B · Facts from the harness

A pulse from the running tool, counters for the run, and fact lines for commits, checks, saved files, work sent back and failures.

Effort: about a week. Tokens: none. Risk: it reads like a machine log if it shows too much. It says what, never why.

C · The worker narrates in passing

The progress tool takes a plan and ticks. A command can carry a few words of intent. The page shows "3 of 5" and the intent as the pulse.

Effort: about a week. Tokens: about 12 per command, no extra requests. Risk: models forget to tick. A changed tool invalidates prompt caches once.

D · A watcher writes the news

After every few steps the harness sends a small model the new steps of one task. It answers with one sentence, or with nothing.

Effort: about two weeks, with an offline test first. Tokens: about 5 cents a day here. Risk: a wrong line that sounds sure. Text from tools can try to steer it.

E · The watcher reads first

The watcher also flags a stuck or looping worker. Routine check-ins by the big model become rare. Nudges wait until a run ends.

Effort: about a week after D. Tokens: saves big-model turns. Risk: a small model misses trouble, so a slow check-in stays as a net.

F · The page settles

From the chatless exploration: the watcher drafts "what you get so far" lines, marked as guesses. The coordinator's check turns them into checked lines.

Effort: weeks, on the next-big branch. Risk: the reader acts on a guess. Text moves while it is read.

How it avoids noise and churn

  • One line per step of the work, not per tool call. The target is one line for every 3 to 5 minutes of work.
  • The current step replaces itself. A line is added when a step ends or an event happens.
  • "Nothing new" is the usual answer. The watcher is told to stay silent when the work has not moved.
  • A cap. At most twelve lines per task per hour. Three show, the rest fold under "Earlier".
  • Bursts merge. Nine saved files are one line.
  • Nothing reflows. New lines arrive in one place with a soft highlight. Ages tick on the device.
  • No notifications, no stale marks, no history rows come from the Live block.
  • It ends with the work. The trail folds into one Activity row when the run ends.
  • No loops. A pass runs after new steps, never on a timer. An idle task costs nothing.
  • Cost follows attention. Passes can run at full rate only while the task is open or on screen.

  • The watcher's input is the new steps, clipped: tool name, a short argument or intent, a short result tail, and the model's own thinking summary. That is about 210,000 tokens a day here.
  • Each pass adds about 500 tokens of rules and page state. About 290 passes a day here, so about 360,000 input and 18,000 output tokens a day.
  • The budget works like the report check's: a daily token cap per project, a named model, and usage shown in settings. When it runs out, the block falls back to facts.
  • Intents cost about 12 output tokens per command and no extra request.
  • More worker reporting is affordable but not reliable. Tripling it would add about 5% to worker spend, and workers would still go quiet when busy.
  • Savings to look for later: 63 of 340 nudged sections were "changes landed on main", which the page already shows as Delivery.

Choices for the owner

1 · Which path?

Live block in stages: A, B, plan, then the watcher recommendedThe page moves after the first week, at no token cost. The watcher comes once it is tested.
Facts and the agents' words onlyStages 1 to 3. No model text on a task. The quiet stretches are filled mechanically.
Watcher firstThe most visible change soonest. It ships before there is a trail and facts to ground it.
Only on next-big, as the settling pageToday's app stays as it is until the big version.

2 · May a small model write on a task?

Yes, in the Live block only, marked auto recommendedOutcome sections stay written by agents.
Yes, also drafts of What you getThe settling page. Each line carries a guess or checked mark.
NoOnly recorded facts and what agents write.

3 · How fine is the grain?

About one line per 3 to 5 minutes of work recommendedRoughly five lines for a typical run.
Finer: about one line a minuteCloser to the chat. More to read, more churn.
Coarser: plan steps and events onlyNo small model needed. Quiet stretches of ten minutes stay.

4 · May the watcher be tested on stored runs?

Yes, on this project's stored worker runs recommendedReal work is the only fair test. The clipped log goes to the small model's provider. Nothing is committed.
Invented runs onlyNothing real leaves the machine. The accuracy number means less.

Limits

  • One project's board and transcripts. Other projects may run fewer or longer workers.
  • No small model was run on the work. Watcher cost and quality are estimates from clipped log sizes.
  • Prices are those in this project's request log. A dearer small model is shown at ten times that.
  • Progress calls made inside scripts were counted as writes. Their phases were read, their other content was not.
  • Android was not drawn. The block is one column and carries over.
  • The replay is scripted. It shows the page, not a working watcher.
